The RBA's early read on AI and jobs

Around three-quarters of firms the RBA talks to say AI hasn't changed their headcount in the past year. The early signs are in who's being hired.

On 9 November, RBA Assistant Governor Sarah Hunter said around 12% of firms in the bank's liaison program reported slower hiring or not filling roles. Around 8% reported reducing headcount, and about 6% reported increasing it.

The same day, an RBA Insight paper by Mike Major found limited labour market disruption from AI so far. Among the occupations most exposed to AI, younger workers appear to have had weaker employment outcomes and larger rises in unemployment than older workers. The paper says these patterns shouldn't immediately be read as evidence of AI displacement, partly because youth employment was unusually strong in the tight market of 2022. Its latest complete occupation data run to February 2026.

If you're running AI adoption, the hiring point is the one I'd keep an eye on. If entry-level work is where AI shows up first, it's worth asking where your junior people will learn the tasks that AI now does.
