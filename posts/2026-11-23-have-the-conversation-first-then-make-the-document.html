Have the conversation first, then make the document

Office in Copilot is now generally available, so a Copilot chat can turn straight into a Word document, a workbook or a deck.

Microsoft says you can go from a conversation to a document, then edit it in the same place with the usual formatting, comments and real-time coauthoring. When you need more, you open it in the full Office app. Files save to OneDrive and keep your Microsoft 365 permissions and sensitivity labels.

One way to try it this week:

1. Pick something you'd normally write from scratch, such as a project update.

2. Tell Copilot who it's for, what they need to decide, and what you know so far.

3. Turn that into the document, then edit it with a colleague.

My view: the conversation is the part worth teaching. "Write me a project update" gets you something generic. Two minutes on the audience and the decision gets you a draft that's much closer to usable.

Think about handing a report to a new colleague. You wouldn't just say "write the update". You'd tell them who reads it, what they need to decide, and what's already known. Copilot needs the same briefing.
