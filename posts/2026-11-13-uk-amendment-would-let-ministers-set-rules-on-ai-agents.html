UK amendment would let ministers set rules on AI agents

A proposed UK amendment would let ministers set rules on how organisations deploy and report on AI agents. It isn't law yet.

The Register reported on 6 November that the amendment was added to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill at its third reading in the House of Lords. It would let ministers regulate how agentic AI is deployed and reported at certain organisations. Those could include gas and electricity suppliers, managed service providers and cloud providers that deploy agents for customers.

The clause would let ministers add investigatory powers, and it doesn't impose duties directly. Foundation model developers wouldn't be covered automatically just for building the underlying technology. It would still need consultation and parliamentary approval through secondary legislation, and the bill itself is still passing through Parliament.

It's a UK proposal, and a long way from a rule anyone has to follow. But the question travels. If you're running AI adoption, could you list the agents running in your organisation, what each can access, and who owns it?
